# ErgoEMA Comprehensive Empirical Evaluation Report

**Dataset**: 14,757 Real-World Front-Facing Video Frames (Primary Scope)
**Total Multi-Angle Corpus**: 52,318 Frames (Front, Oblique, Lateral)
**Study Cohort**: 11 De-Identified Participants (`Participant1` through `Participant11`)
**Methodology**: Online Personalized Calibration + Exponential Moving Average (EMA)
**Workstation Standard**: ISO 9241-5 Compliant Anterior Desktop Webcam Monitoring

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Core Results

The **ErgoEMA** architecture provides real-time ergonomic posture monitoring using standard monocular webcams without specialized hardware. It integrates:
1. Scale-invariant front-facing biometrics ($R_{H2S}$ normalized by biacromial span),
2. Rapid 3-second online personalized upright baseline calibration ($\boldsymbol{\mu}_{base}, \boldsymbol{\sigma}_{base}$), and
3. Recursive $O(1)$ multi-channel Exponential Moving Average (EMA) temporal filtering.

On participants held out of tuning (Leave-One-Subject-Out cross-validation), ErgoEMA reaches **74.96% accuracy**, **69.87% F1-score** and a **22.58% False Alarm Rate** across the 11-participant front-facing cohort, with per-participant F1 ranging from 14.43% (Participant 4) to 99.39% (Participant 1). Side-on, alerting on the drop from the participant's calibrated upright craniovertebral angle reaches **97.61% accuracy** with a 1.69% FAR. The EMA update and classification step takes a median of **0.0128 ms** per frame on the machine that generated these results, excluding MediaPipe pose estimation. One front-view recording, filmed side-on, is excluded from the dataset (Section 3).

All figures in Sections 2–6 are generated by `train.py`; the source of record is [`training_thesis_report.md`](training_thesis_report.md).

## 2. Benchmark Architecture Comparison (Primary Front-Facing Dataset)

Evaluated across **14,757 front-facing video frames** under LOSO: each participant is predicted by a model tuned or trained on the other 10 participants. Latency is the measured median per-frame classification time (pose estimation excluded):

| Model Architecture | Accuracy (%) | Precision (%) | Recall (%) | F1-Score (%) | FAR (%) | Latency (ms) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **ErgoEMA (Adaptive Time-Series)** | **`74.96%`** | **`68.44%`** | **`71.37%`** | **`69.87%`** | **`22.58%`** | **`0.0128 ms`** |
| Random Forest (100 Trees) | 51.18% | 42.14% | 53.56% | 47.17% | 50.45% | 6.3361 ms |
| Support Vector Machine (RBF) | 53.64% | 42.86% | 41.89% | 42.37% | 38.31% | 1.0897 ms |
| Logistic Regression | 65.30% | 63.34% | 34.91% | 45.01% | 13.86% | 0.1279 ms |
| Static Rigid Threshold | 53.11% | 42.96% | 46.47% | 44.64% | 42.33% | 0.0001 ms |

> **Key Observation**: The machine-learning baselines, which classify raw feature values, transfer poorly to unseen participants (42–47% held-out F1, 51–65% accuracy). The threshold rule, which scores each frame relative to the participant's own calibrated baseline, has the highest held-out accuracy and F1-score. Logistic Regression has the lowest False Alarm Rate (13.86%) but detects only 34.91% of poor-posture frames, against 71.37% for the threshold rule. The forward-head depth threshold $\delta_{FHP}$ is tuned with $\alpha$ and $\delta_{slouch}$. With it fixed at its former value of 0.06, the same protocol gives 75.49% accuracy, 71.80% F1 and a 25.33% FAR: tuning it lowers the FAR but, on this cohort, also slightly lowers accuracy and F1.

## 4. Camera Viewpoint Sensitivity & Perspective Ablation

Evaluated across the complete multi-perspective corpus of **52,318 frames** (52,021 scored). Every row runs the configuration the live app uses: the front-view hyperparameters ($\alpha = 0.08$, $\delta_{slouch} = 8\%$, $\delta_{FHP} = 0.20$) for frontal and oblique frames, and the side-on settings ($\alpha = 0.25$, alert at a CVA drop of $5^\circ$) for frames detected as lateral. The Front and Lateral rows are in-sample (tuned on those frames); the Oblique row is not.

| Camera Perspective | Angle | Frames | Accuracy (%) | Precision (%) | Recall (%) | F1-Score (%) | FAR (%) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Front View (Primary Scope)** | $0^\circ$ | 14,757 | 80.27% | 75.66% | 75.95% | 75.80% | 16.76% |
| **Oblique Profile** | $45^\circ$ | 32,752 | 53.06% | 82.50% | 8.90% | 16.06% | 1.92% |
| **Lateral Profile** | $90^\circ$ | 4,512 | 97.67% | 98.52% | 96.95% | 97.73% | 1.56% |
| **All Combined Perspectives** | All | 52,021 | 63.26% | 74.99% | 34.70% | 47.45% | 10.60% |

With the front-view settings, a $45^\circ$ camera catches only 8.90% of poor-posture frames: the tuned forward-head threshold rarely triggers at that angle. In the All Combined row each front-view participant is calibrated once on their first upright frames, and that calibration also serves their oblique frames; the side-on participants, who are numbered separately, are calibrated on their own side-on upright frames. The 297 public benchmark images in the lateral dataset carry no participant ID and are not scored.

### Perspective Analysis:
1. **Front View ($0^\circ$)**: Monocular frontal cameras maintain full visibility of both acromion shoulder landmarks, allowing scale-invariant head-to-shoulder ratio computation.
2. **Oblique Profile ($45^\circ$)**: Suffer from perspective foreshortening that artificially alters apparent biacromial distance.
3. **Lateral Profile ($90^\circ$)**: The estimated craniovertebral angle (CVA), with C7 located on the back-of-neck silhouette, is compared with the participant's calibrated upright CVA. Forward Head Posture is named only when the CVA is also below the clinical $50^\circ$ criterion. Thoracic kyphosis cannot be measured from video, so slouch is not assessed in this view.

Most remaining errors are missed frames of Participants 1 and 4 (recall 60.38% and 57.89%).
